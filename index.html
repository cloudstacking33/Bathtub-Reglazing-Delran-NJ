What Bathtub Reglazing Really Is

You may hear several names for the same basic service: bathtub reglazing, bathtub refinishing, tub reglazing, bathtub resurfacing, or bathtub restoration. All of them describe renewing the surface of a fixture so it looks and performs like new. Some people call it bathtub painting, but professional reglazing is very different from a rolled on coat of household paint.

A proper reglaze begins with deep cleaning to remove soap scum, residue, and old coatings. Our technicians then repair chips, cracks, and rough areas so the surface is even. Next comes a professional bonding agent, followed by several layers of durable, professional grade coating. Within 24 to 48 hours, the finish cures into a smooth, glossy shell that resists peeling, staining, and everyday wear. The same approach works on tile, showers, sinks, vanities, Jacuzzis, and spas.

Our Materials and Standards

The coatings we use are chosen for performance and for the comfort of your household. They are low VOC with less smell, contain zero isocyanates, and are bleach resistant and durable. With proper care, a professionally reglazed surface can last 10 years or more, and our work is backed by a warranty.

We also care about how a job feels from your side. Technicians arrive on time, protect the work area, and keep the process tidy. Customers regularly mention how neat, friendly, and knowledgeable our team is, and we take that feedback seriously. Quality finishes start with careful preparation, so we never rush the steps that matter most.

Reglazing vs Replacement vs Bathtub Liners

A new bathtub can seem like the obvious fix for a worn tub, but the full cost is often surprising. Installation, plumbing work, and debris removal can often double the price, and you may lose several days of bathroom use. A bathtub liner is another option people consider, but it is an insert that must be fitted precisely and can leave seams. Reglazing restores the tub itself with a seamless finish and keeps the original fixture in place.

We are not afraid to be honest. If a tub is cracked through or structurally damaged, replacement may be the better path, and we will say so. When the issue is a stained, chipped, rusted, or dull surface, reglazing usually delivers the best value. It also keeps a heavy fixture out of the landfill, which is better for the environment.
